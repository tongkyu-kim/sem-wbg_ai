The repository serves three purposes, described in detail below.

## Background and Motivation

### 1. Coding Sample for Quantitative Research Teams

A primary motivation for this repository is to demonstrate quantitative, statistical, and programming competency. While development economics research — including the work of teams such as WBG DIME — often centres on experimental and quasi-experimental methods, the underlying requirements are broadly shared: rigorous data management, reproducible analytical workflows, statistical modelling, and theory-driven empirical analysis.

This repository illustrates those capabilities in the context of a survey-based SEM study. Although SEM is methodologically distinct from impact evaluation, the research pipeline — cleaning and validating raw survey data, implementing multi-step estimation procedures, conducting sensitivity analyses, and producing publication-ready outputs — closely reflects the demands of applied quantitative research roles.

Python was chosen deliberately over dedicated SEM software (e.g., Stata, R `lavaan`, Mplus). Implementing the workflow from first principles in Python required explicit handling of factor loading estimation, score construction, path estimation, bootstrap resampling, and formatted output — all of which are transparent and reviewable in the notebook.

### 2. Replication Package for the KGGTF Youth Intern Program Research Project

This repository is the replication package for a survey-based research project conducted under the Korea Green Growth Trust Fund (KGGTF) Youth Intern Program (YIP). It provides project collaborators with:

- the full analytical workflow from raw data to results tables and figures;
- documented methodological decisions (item exclusions, estimation strategy, threshold conventions); and
- reproducible outputs that can be regenerated by running a single notebook.

### 3. Learning Resource for Researchers and Practitioners

The repository is intended as an open reference for researchers, students, and practitioners interested in conducting SEM analyses in Python. The notebook is structured to follow the standard two-stage measurement-then-structural workflow (Anderson & Gerbing 1988), with each analysis section clearly separated, documented, and linked to relevant methodological citations. Users are welcome to adapt the code structure, workflow, documentation, and reporting format for their own projects.

## Research Design

**Research question**: Do AI risk awareness and environmental awareness predict responsible and environmentally motivated AI-use behaviors, and does attitude toward AI mediate these relationships?

**Sample**: Self-administered online survey, young adults in South Korea (N = 117, zero missing values).

**Construct model** (five reflective constructs):

| Construct | Items | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Awareness (Worry) | 4 | Perceived risk of AI errors, ethics violations, privacy breaches, and deepfake misuse |
| Awareness (Env) | 2 | Perceived environmental impact of AI data centres and cumulative AI use |
| Attitude | 3 | Overall positive attitude toward AI use |
| Beh\_Environmental | 2 | Environmentally motivated AI avoidance and advisory behaviour |
| Beh\_Responsible | 4 | Responsible AI-use behaviour: verification, harm-checking, efficient prompting |

**Hypothesised structural model**:

```
Awareness (Worry) ──┐
                    ▼
Awareness (Env)  ──→  Attitude  ──→  Beh_Environmental
                               └──→  Beh_Responsible
```

## Analysis Pipeline

The notebook (`notebooks/Structural Equation Modelling.ipynb`) is organized into eight sequential sections. Each section is self-contained and documented with inline method descriptions and literature citations.

| Section | Content | Key Methods / References |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Descriptive Analysis** | Demographic profile, AI service usage, Likert item statistics (mean, SD, skewness, kurtosis) | Frequency tables, item-level descriptives |
| **2. Convergent Validity** | Internal consistency and construct reliability per indicator | Cronbach's α; Composite Reliability (Dillon-Goldstein ρc); Dijkstra-Henseler ρA; AVE (Fornell & Larcker 1981) |
| **3. Discriminant Validity** | Between-construct distinctiveness | Fornell-Larcker criterion; HTMT ratios (Henseler, Ringle & Sarstedt 2015) |
| **4. Common Method Bias** | Three-test CMB screening on composite scores | Harman's single-factor test; full-collinearity VIF (Kock & Lynn 2012); marker variable test (Lindell & Whitney 2001) |
| **5. Structural Equation Modeling** | Two-stage measurement + structural estimation | CFA fit indices via semopy (MLW); Bartlett (1937) factor scores; OLS structural paths — equivalent to PLS-SEM (Anderson & Gerbing 1988; Hair et al. 2019) |
| **6. Mediation Analysis** | Attitude as mediator across all four X → Y combinations | Sobel test (Baron & Kenny 1986); percentile bootstrap CI (Preacher & Hayes 2008; B = 5,000) |
| **7. Multigroup Analysis** | Path coefficient comparison across demographic subgroups (Gender, Field, Usage Frequency) | Within-group OLS path estimation; z-test for coefficient equality (Paternoster et al. 1998) |
| **8. SEM Path Diagram** | Visualized model with β, significance stars, and R² | matplotlib FancyBboxPatch + annotate |

### Note on estimation strategy

Full covariance-based SEM (CB-SEM) requires large samples to stabilize the Fisher Information Matrix. With N = 117 and 15 indicators across five constructs, the optimizer encounters lower-bound constraints on latent variable variances, producing unreliable structural path estimates. Following Hair et al. (2019), a two-stage approach is adopted:

1. **Stage 1** — CFA fit indices are reported to confirm adequate measurement model fit (χ²/df, CFI, TLI, RMSEA).
2. **Stage 2** — Bartlett (1937) factor scores (weighted composites derived from ML-FA loadings) are used as latent variable proxies, and structural paths are estimated via OLS regression. This is the factor-score equivalent of PLS-SEM.

## Replication Workflow

### Prerequisites

- Python 3.9 or later
- Jupyter Notebook or JupyterLab

### Installation

```bash
git clone https://github.com/donkeekim/sem.git
cd sem
pip install -r requirements.txt
```

### Running the analysis

```bash
jupyter notebook "notebooks/Structural Equation Modelling.ipynb"
```

Run all cells in order (`Kernel → Restart & Run All`). The notebook will:

1. Load the raw survey data from `src/raw/wbg_ai.xlsx`.
2. Execute all eight analysis sections sequentially.
3. Write dated output files to `output/tables/`, `output/stats/`, `output/figures/`, and `output/` (Word report).

Output filenames follow the pattern `<name>_YYYYMMDD.*` using the execution date. Re-running on a different date will produce a new set of files without overwriting prior outputs.

### Selective replication

Each section of the notebook is independent after the setup cell (which loads the data, defines constructs, and defines helper functions). Individual sections can be re-run in isolation by re-executing the setup cell first.

## Limitations and Disclaimers

**Sample size.** N = 117 is below the recommended minimum for full CB-SEM with complex models (Hair et al. 2019 suggest N ≥ 200 as a general guide). The two-stage factor-score approach is adopted as a principled alternative, but results should be interpreted with appropriate caution regarding stability and generalizability.

**Sample composition.** The sample is predominantly female (70.1%), aged 25–29 (74.4%), and STEM-educated (50.4%), reflecting the characteristics of the KGGTF YIP cohort rather than the general young adult population. Findings may not generalize beyond this group.

**Cross-sectional design.** All constructs were measured at a single point in time. Causal interpretations of structural paths and mediated effects are theoretical rather than empirically established.

**Self-reported data.** All items are based on self-report, introducing the possibility of social desirability and recall biases. Common method bias was assessed via three complementary tests (Harman, VIF, marker variable), with no major concern identified, though the marker variable test flagged one borderline pair.

**AVE thresholds.** Three of five constructs do not meet the AVE ≥ 0.50 threshold (Fornell & Larcker 1981). This is noted and discussed in the notebook. Composite reliability (CR ≥ 0.70) is met or closely approached for all constructs, and HTMT-based discriminant validity is fully supported.

**Multigroup analysis.** With n = 35 males and n = 44 light users, within-group path estimates for these subgroups should be interpreted with caution. No statistically significant group differences were detected, though this may partly reflect limited statistical power in smaller subgroups.

**Replication scope.** This repository replicates the quantitative analysis only. Survey instrument design, data collection procedures, and qualitative interpretation are documented separately in the project report.
